# Project guidance

## Source-code Easter eggs

Maintain a subtle, recurring layer of source-code Easter eggs for engineers and technically curious readers who inspect the portfolio source.

- Preserve the opening HTML comment beginning “Whoa! You're looking under the covers! You must be an engineer.” as the opening of the hidden narrative.
- Keep roughly 90% of the source professional and conventional. Easter eggs should be rare enough that each discovery feels intentional.
- Use a dry, understated, self-aware voice that is affectionate toward engineers. Never make the humor mocking, cute, loud, or disruptive.
- Draw primarily from designer–engineer diplomacy and woodworking/building metaphors: intentional pixels, visual judgment, implementation compromises, shims, patches, square, flush, proud, kerf, rails, joinery, dry fitting, grain direction, measure twice/cut once, load-bearing pieces, and technically sound joints.
- Prefer comments and, only where harmless and clear, CSS custom-property names. Do not rename established classes, IDs, functions, variables, or other structural identifiers for a joke.
- Never change visual output, behavior, accessibility, semantics, SEO, performance, readability, or maintainability to create an Easter egg.
- Do not force an Easter egg into every file or every change. Add one only when the surrounding implementation creates a natural opportunity.
- Avoid repeating existing jokes. Treat the source reader as smart; reward discovery without explaining the punchline.
- Keep future additions consistent with the voice and restraint of the Easter eggs already present in the HTML and CSS.
